## Issues

### R1 · step3_stats.py:116-119, 128-129 · The §6 "2022-only" refutation rule is not enforced · **critical** · confidence high
- §6 says the claim is **refuted** if "the effect is present only in the 2022 cohort, i.e. present in 2022 and absent in 2021 when tested separately". The code runs the cohort analyses, but `OVERALL_SUPPORTED` never uses them.
- The per-cohort H2 line is printed only when `OVERALL_SUPPORTED is False` (line 128). That is the opposite of when it matters: it should be checked when the overall verdict is "supported".
- **Demonstrated** (`tools/mutate2.py`, 2021 dv mean 0.002): the 2021 cohort has H2 p = 0.36 with 0/15 sessions above null, and the 2022 cohort has p = 0.031 with 6/6 above null. The primary verdict is `OVERALL_SUPPORTED: True`, nothing about cohorts is printed, and the 2022-only status is reported nowhere.
- This is the main confound already known for these data (strongly coupled 2022 sessions). Note also that "above null" (R4) is satisfied by the 6 sessions of 2022 alone.
- Fix: compute the cohort verdicts unconditionally and define "present" and "absent" in the amendment. A suggestion: present means the cohort H2 has median > 0 and p < 0.05; absent means the 2021 cohort is not present. Then set `OVERALL_SUPPORTED = False` with the reason "2022-only" when 2022 is present and 2021 is absent. Write the cohort verdicts into the VERDICT row. The 2022 cohort has n = 6, so its Wilcoxon reaches p < 0.05 only when all 6 sessions share a sign (minimum two-sided p = 0.031). State that in the amendment.

### R2 · step3_stats.py:87, 98 (and A7) · A significantly *negative* held-out κ gain refutes fixed J · **major** · confidence high
- A7 contains two different rules. The first: "Wilcoxon on session means, two-sided, both p > 0.05". The second: "a significant κ̂ or a **positive** held-out gain refutes". The code follows the first: `h3b_ok = p_kappa > .05 and p_gain > .05`, with no sign condition on the gain. §5 H3(b) says the κ model "must not **improve** held-out likelihood".
- Under fixed J the κ model's held-out gain is expected to be slightly **negative**, because it fits one extra parameter. Step 1 confirms this: in scenario A the mean gain was −0.0004, −0.0003, +0.0001 and −0.0003 across the four (N, R) cells, and the gain was positive in only 7 of 20 replicates (`step1_summary.csv`). A small but consistent negative gain across 21 sessions gives two-sided p < 0.05, so the code declares "fixed J refuted". That is the wrong conclusion.
- **Demonstrated:** with `neg_gain` (gain ~ N(−0.0003, 0.0003) in a fixture that is otherwise "supported"), the result is `H3b_no_change: False` and the refuted reason is "kappa-hat or held-out gain shows a coupling change". The default `supported` fixture (seed 1) also fails this way by chance: gain median −0.00016, p = 0.038.
- Fix: keep κ̂ two-sided, since a change of J in either direction refutes fixed J. Refute on the gain only if p < 0.05 **and** median > 0, or use a one-sided "greater" test. Write this into a dated A7 clarification before reading results.
- Also note that requiring both tests to give p > 0.05 means a true fixed J is rejected about 10% of the time (two tests at 5% each). That is a property of an absence test, but it should be stated in the amendment.

### R3 · step3_stats.py:95, 97 · H3a's decision uses raw p; its BH q is computed but never used · **medium** · confidence high
- A7 enters the H3a p-value into the {H2, H3, H4a} BH family. But `h3a_ok` tests `R["H3a"]["p"] < .05`, and `q_H3a` affects no decision. H2 and H4a use q, so H3 is the only family member that ignores the FDR correction.
- A7's H3a sentence ("Spearman ρ > 0 … with p < 0.05") can be read either way, and §6 does not attach p_FDR to H3. In practice the difference only matters when p is between 0.017 and 0.05, but the rule should be pinned down.
- Fix: use `q[1] < .05`. Otherwise, the amendment should say explicitly that H3a is gated on raw p, which would make its entry in BH serve only to correct H2 and H4a.

### R4 · step3_stats.py:74-76 · The H2 "above own null" approximation: conservative in practice but not guaranteed, and the gate is weak · **medium** · confidence high (simulation)
- **Is it conservative?** The A7 bound is the mean over conditions (and subsets) of the per-task 95th percentile. The pre-registered quantity is the 95th percentile over b of the session-averaged null. Each shuffle of each task is independent, so averaging shrinks the null distribution toward its mean. The mean of quantiles stays at the per-task spread. When the per-task nulls are jointly elliptical (for example Gaussian), the 95% quantile is subadditive, so the mean of the p95s is ≥ the p95 of the mean. Equality holds only under comonotonicity. **This is not a general guarantee.** The 95% quantile is not subadditive for skewed or discrete heavy-tailed distributions.
- Simulation (`tools/nullsim.py`; 3 conditions, 20 shuffles, 20,000 sessions). The table gives the share of sessions where the approximate bound is below the exact one, and the per-session false-positive rate (size) of each test.

  | null shape | layout | P(approx < exact) | size, approx | size, exact |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Gaussian | 3 × 1 | 0.005 | 0.020 | 0.092 |
  | Gaussian | 3 × 10 subsets | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.093 |
  | lognormal | 3 × 1 | 0.066 | 0.050 | 0.090 |
  | t₂ | 3 × 1 | 0.059 | 0.045 | 0.090 |
  | rare spike (4% mass) | 3 × 1 | 0.27 | **0.082** | 0.080 |
  | rare spike | 3 × 10 | 0.051 | 0.022 | 0.079 |

  For continuous nulls the approximation is conservative. For sessions with N > 20 it is **extremely** conservative: the bound is about 4–5× the exact p95, so those 7 sessions will almost never count as above null. With sparse or spiky nulls it can fail to be conservative.
- With only 20 shuffles, even the exact test has a per-session size of about 9%, not 5%. `np.percentile(·, 95)` of 20 values interpolates at order statistic 19.05, and P(real > X₍₁₉₎) = 2/21. So the binomial reference p₀ = 0.05 suits the approximate bound, but it would be anti-conservative if the exact null were substituted later.
- **The gate itself is weak.** The one-sided binomial against 5% passes with **4 of 21** sessions above their bound (3 of 15 in 2021, 2 of 6 in 2022). The pre-registration's "the real session ΔI must exceed the 95th percentile of its own null" reads much more strictly. A7 changed it legitimately, before any results, but the amendment should report the count and say plainly that 4/21 passes. Together with R1, the 2022 sessions alone can satisfy this gate.
- Reporting gap: neither the per-session bound nor the above/below flag is written out (`e10_sessions.csv`, line 124), so readers cannot see which sessions passed. Add `null_bound` and `above_null` columns.

### R5 · step3_stats.py (absent) · The exact null for the N = 19 sessions is never computed · **minor** · confidence high

Sessions 220515 and 220516 consist entirely of N = 19 conditions, so their exact session-level null can be computed. A7 does **not** require it in the primary analysis (A7's primary is the approximation for all sessions), so the code is consistent with A7. ### R6 · step3_stats.py:31, 71, 90 · NaNs are averaged out silently at the subset→condition and condition→session steps · **minor** · confidence high
- `groupby(...).mean()` skips NaN. A NaN `real_dI` in one task is replaced by the mean of the remaining subsets or conditions. The only signal is the generic "NaN in column real_dI: 1 tasks" warning, which doesn't say that session 210421's mean now uses 2 of 3 conditions. The `complete` flag doesn't account for NaNs.
- Demonstrated (`nan_dI`): the session-level Wilcoxon shows no drop, while the H4a mixed model fails completely ("factor contains missing values"), so `mixed_slope` is NaN in every analysis set.
- Fix: count NaNs per condition and per session in the warnings, mark those conditions `complete = False`, and pass `missing="drop"` to `mixedlm`.

### R7 · step3_stats.py:65-67 · One NaN p-value makes every BH q NaN · **minor** · confidence high
- `np.minimum.accumulate` propagates NaN (checked: `[0.01, 0.02, nan]` gives all NaN). If `wil` returns p = NaN (n < 6 or all zeros) or the Spearman p is NaN, all three q-values become NaN and every verdict becomes False without explanation. This is unlikely in the primary set but possible in subsets. Fix: put NaN p-values into BH as 1, or warn when one occurs.

### R8 · step3_stats.py:19-21, 100 · A verdict is produced even with whole sessions missing · **minor** · confidence high
- Demonstrated (`drop_session`): with session 210423 deleted, the script warns three times and still prints `OVERALL_SUPPORTED: True` on n = 20. That is not silent, so it meets A7's "lists anything missing". But the VERDICT row doesn't record n, and nothing stops the run. Suggest writing `n_sessions` and `n_warnings` into the VERDICT row, or refusing to print a primary verdict unless `--allow-incomplete` is given.
- Also, extra subsets beyond `n_expected_tasks` are not flagged (only `n < expected` is checked).

### R9 · step3_stats.py:24-25 · Convergence is not reported per cohort · **minor** · confidence high
- A7(b) says "Real and κ convergence are reported per cohort". The code lists the non-converged tasks but produces no per-cohort counts. A one-line groupby fixes it.

### R10 · test_step3_synthetic.py · The fixtures have no assertions, and the planted verdict fails at the default seed · **minor** · confidence high
- The script only generates data, and no expected verdict is checked. Results:

  | Scenario (seed 1) | Planted verdict | Script's verdict | Correct? |
