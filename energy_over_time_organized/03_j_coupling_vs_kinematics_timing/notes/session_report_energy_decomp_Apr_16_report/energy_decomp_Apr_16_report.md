## J-Jump vs Kinematic Peak Analysis

*Added: 2026-04-18*

**Question:** Do rapid changes (jumps) in J (mean neural coupling) align with peak kinematic events? Does J lead or lag the movement?

**Method:** For each session, the *J jump* is defined as the time bin of maximum |dJ/dt| within the search window — where coupling changes fastest. This is compared to the peak acceleration and peak velocity indices. Lag = j\_jump\_idx − kinematic\_peak\_idx (negative = J precedes kinematics = J *leads*; positive = J *lags*).

### Interpretation

**J lags acceleration but aligns with velocity.** Across all 63 sessions and all 3 stimuli, the J jump (largest rapid change in mean neural coupling) occurs approximately **3–4 bins after the acceleration peak**, but is nearly **simultaneous with the velocity peak** (mean lag ≈ +0.3 bins). Since velocity is the integral of acceleration, its peak comes slightly later — J transitions at the moment of **peak movement speed**, not at movement onset.

**The 43:17 lag-to-lead ratio is consistent across all stimuli** (roughly 2.5:1 in every condition), arguing against a simple causal role of coupling in *initiating* movement. Instead, J appears to reorganize *at* or *just after* the peak kinematic event.

**Pearson correlations are near zero** (|r| < 0.1 in almost all sessions). J does not track the continuous shape of either kinematic signal — the finding is about *transition timing*, not sustained co-variation.

**Notable observations:**
- **220518, Stim 1** has the largest J jump in the dataset (|ΔJ| = 3.57) and is one of the few sessions where J *leads* acceleration. Large coupling transitions may behave differently.
- **210614** lags by +10–13 bins consistently across all stimuli — a systematically delayed coupling response in this session.
- Sessions with very small |ΔJ| (< 0.1) have negligible coupling transitions and may not be meaningful for this analysis.
- The cross-correlation lags (XC\_A, XC\_V) span ±400 bins and should be treated with caution given the near-zero Pearson correlations; the extremum-based signed lag is the reliable metric here.
